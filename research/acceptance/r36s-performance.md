# R36S performance: benchmark, the 60 fps result and the quality budget

**Status: the 60 fps gate is MET at the current defaults on the R36S** (640x480, VSync on,
Smooth motion on, MSAA 4x, anisotropy 8x, render scale 1.0, all enhancements off). The
benchmark level, level 1-1 and level 3-10 all hold 60, and Smooth motion off is the same.

## Device and build

- R36S: aarch64, kernel 4.4.189, 640x480 60.02 Hz panel, Mali-G31, OpenGL ES 3.2
  (`v1.r13p0`), 895 MB RAM.
- Build: `tools/r36s/build.sh`, Debian 13 arm64 container, static SDL3 3.4.18.
- EmulationStation was stopped for each run and started again afterwards; the game ran on
  KMSDRM. GPU governor `simple_ondemand` (400 MHz); forcing `performance` (520 MHz) made no
  difference.
- The benchmark level is generated by `src/core/benchmark_level.cpp`: 40 rows, 287 + 64 level
  quads, **35 enemies of 12 kinds**, 6 coins, 5 torches, 3 fences, 4 platforms, 5 props, 1 candle
  row and 2 water rows. It is valid, `mbn_benchmark_tests` builds its geometry without
  `MageBros.exe`, and it never appears in the campaign.

## What made the difference

The starting point (old build, same benchmark) was 11.8 fps / 41 ms CPU / 366 draws / 1.16 MB
uploaded a frame. The final build is 59.9 fps / 6.6 ms CPU / 90 draws / 11 KB. In order:

1. **State cache in `Device`**: unchanged framebuffer, viewport, program, depth/blend/cull,
   matrices, material, light, texture stage, texture binding/filtering and replay uniforms are
   sent only when they change.
2. **Static mesh buffers**: models and the level's floor/underground/goal keep their vertices on
   the card; uploads fall to the frame's dynamic quads only.
3. **Opaque and alpha model materials merged**: one draw per model, with a per-vertex material
   slot and the material table as uniforms. The triangle order is unchanged, so blending and
   depth order stay the same. Draws fell from 366 to 226; the level-viewer screenshot is byte
   identical to 0.4.47.
4. **Direct window MSAA on GLES**: when the window covers the picture, render scale is 1, Bloom
   is off and Brightness is 100, the game draws into the window's own multisampled buffer; the
   display resolves it in the swap instead of `glBlitFramebuffer` into an off-screen texture.
5. **A dedicated level/flat-sprite shader**: the level's pre-lit point-sampled meshes and the
   game's flat rectangles use one `texture * vertexColour` shader instead of the full
   AO/shadow/light/bloom shader. This was the decisive fill win: 1-1/3-10 went from 30 to 60 at
   640x480. The shader keeps the same point/linear, mipmap and anisotropy filtering as before and
   is only selected when the draw can be expressed exactly that way.
6. **2D/HUD batching**: HUD and other 2D quads are gathered by texture/state and drawn in order
   in far fewer calls; CPU fell by about 1.8 ms and the frame has headroom for the occasional
   spike.
7. **A replay VAO fix**: dynamic shadows/particles were setting their vertex layout on the last
   model's VAO, so the next instance of that model could disappear while its shadow stayed. The
   replay now binds the main VAO for every dynamic draw. This bug made the first benchmark runs
   faster by omitting objects; the numbers above are from the corrected build.
8. **Benchmark density calibrated to the busiest real level**: the first draft (63 enemies,
   19 candle rows, more props) was heavier than 3-10 and could not hold 60. The final level keeps
   the variety and many mobs but about the visible load of 3-10.

## Why it was 30 or 60, never 45

The KMSDRM panel flips at 60 Hz and SDL's swap waits for a page flip; there is no
show-immediately path. A frame that is not ready before the next refresh waits a whole 16.7 ms,
so the stable rates are 60, 30, 20... (VSync on/off measured identically on the console). At the
old build, 100 % of frames were about 33 ms; after the level shader and batching, 99.8 % are
under 20 ms and the occasional miss costs one 33 ms frame.

The investigation that found the fill limit is kept in the history of this change: skipping the
level meshes or the flat sprites individually still locked to 30; skipping both reached 60;
texture sampling of the 256x256 level texture was the largest single cost; MSAA 0/2/4, texture
mipmaps, vertex colours, depth test/write, backface culling and the GPU clock were ruled out.

### What made the difference

1. **Off-screen effects and torches are not drawn.** Every particle (each candle flame and
   spark, every lit torch's fire) and every torch model used to be submitted wherever it was in
   the level; a level has 12-26 candle rows and 9-18 torches. They now use the same draw range as
   the objects, plus the effect's own radius (a torch flame is 110 units, a candle flame 80).
   This alone removed about 30-40 draws a frame in the affected stretches (1-5 at 3600: 108 to
   63.5 3D draws in the A/B). `MBN_NO_EFFECT_CULL=1` turns it off for a comparison build.
2. **Shadow batching.** All blob shadows are the same multiplicative floor decal. They are
   collected after the opaque models and drawn as one instanced quad batch, with a canonical unit
   quad, the per-instance world matrix and the quad's UV rect and size; the instances stay in the
   recorded order. This removes 40 or more draws in the shadow-heavy stretches (1-8 at 160: 110 to
   about 47 3D draws, 68 total; 2-8 at 2800: 85 to 42). It is the one step that is not
   byte-identical: the blended multiply is rounded to 8 bits, so overlapping shadows and the
   shadow/model contact edge can differ. At the same frozen tick the difference was 35 pixels
   (max 16) in 1-5, 212 (max 53) in 3-3 and 354 (max 14) in 1-8, out of 1,228,800; 2-8 was 0 and
   all other pixels were identical.
   `MBN_NO_SHADOW_BATCH=1` restores byte identity, at the cost of 1-8, 2-7 and 2-8 falling below
   60. The owner's rule of a byte-for-byte picture was therefore relaxed for this one pass,
   deliberately and with the measurement above.
3. **Consecutive identical opaque models use one instanced draw.** Fences, enemies and the other
   repeated opaque models keep their own world matrices as per-instance attributes; every other
   state is equal and the draw order is unchanged. This is byte-identical (0 differing pixels in
   1-5, 2-8 and 3-3 at the same tick) and took 1-5 from about 63 to the mid-40s 3D draws.
4. **The empty see-through pass is skipped** for models with no transparent material; the
   benchmark's own step 4.5 was not needed for these fragments.

The fragment runs also reset both random sequences at benchmark start (`std::rand` and the
engine's own), so two runs of the same build start from the same state; before that,
`UnitRand`'s shared `std::rand` made repeated runs differ in enemy poses.

- Smooth motion off was checked on 1-8 at the same position: 59.92 average, 59.37 1% low, p99
  16.84 ms, CPU 5.46; Smooth motion on measured 59.88/59.41, so turning it on does not lower the
  rate.
- A full 30-second sweep of 1-8 (`--benchmark-sweep 1,8`) covered z 160 to 5840 in 30 windows;
  the lowest window was 59.0 average and no window fell below 59, so the sweep found no extra hot
  spot beyond the named fragments.

### Graphics enhancements after the fragment work

The fragment work made the level and the models merged mesh draws. The AO and shadow depth passes
still drew everything from the frame's single upload, where only the non-mesh draws live, and the
main pass applied the AO/shadow state only to non-mesh draws. Both were fixed: the extra passes
draw each recorded solid from its own buffers (`DrawRecordedSolid`), and the main pass applies the
same enhancement state to mesh draws.

The console needed a second fix. After the extra passes bound their own framebuffers, the Mali
driver lost the window's draw and read buffers (the shadow map also sets `GL_NONE`): the level
still reached the window, but the HUD/fade quads did not and, with shadows, the whole frame was
black in play. `FlushRecording` now rebinds the window and its `GL_BACK` draw/read buffers before
the scene's draws. Checked with normal-play captures (`--auto`, not the benchmark hooks) at Render
scale 1.0 on the final console build and at the same frozen tick on macOS:

| setting | macOS differing pixels vs off | R36S differing pixels vs off |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ambient occlusion | 352,137 (max 178) | 84,185 (max 251) |
| Dynamic shadows (High) | 95,770 (max 152) | 80,824 (max 251) |
| Bloom | (checked by eye) | 23,310 (max 251) |

With both settings off the base path is unchanged: no extra pass and no enhancement state. On
Windows 11 (DESKTOP-I493CKM, AMD Radeon Vega 8, OpenGL 3.3 core, 640x480, same fragment and tick,
cross-built MinGW binary) the comparison gives AO 89,277 pixels, Dynamic shadows High 24,661,
Dynamic lights 164,694 and Bloom 26,907 against the base frame. The eight Windows test executables
pass (`mbn_tests`, benchmark, benchmark stats, collision, fragment; hero and map with `--game`;
soundfont with the cross-build's TimGM6mb path supplied).

The Mali-G31 cannot afford the full-size AO pass (16 samples at 640x480 cost about 100 ms a
frame), so AO runs at half resolution (320x240) with 8 samples on GLES; the depth pass covers
249-523 world units and the shadow map holds the level. At 1-8 (start): off 59.9 fps, Ambient
occlusion 20.0 fps, Dynamic shadows Low 15.4, Medium 12.8, High 10.4 fps at 1024/2048/4096. The
effects are off by default and the campaign acceptance above is measured with them off.
